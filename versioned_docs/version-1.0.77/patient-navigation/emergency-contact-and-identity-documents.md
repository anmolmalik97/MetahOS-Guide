# Next of Kin & Identity Documents

This page covers the emergency contact and next-of-kin details captured at registration, and how identity documents with an expiry date — such as a Labour ID — are tracked.

## At a glance

```steps
fa-user | Register the patient | Capture who the patient is.
fa-phone | Emergency contact | Who to call if something goes wrong.
fa-users | Next of kin | The relative responsible for the patient.
fa-id-card | Identity document | Record the ID the patient presents.
fa-calendar | Expiry date | Note when that ID stops being valid.
fa-exclamation-triangle | Expiry warning | Staff are warned when it has lapsed.
```

## Emergency contact and next of kin

The registration form captures, as an optional block:

- **Emergency contact** — a name and a contact number for whoever should be reached in an emergency.
- **Next of kin** — the relative or guardian formally responsible for the patient, and their relationship to them.

Leaving the block empty stores nothing, so it does not get in the way of a fast registration.

:::tip
For an unaccompanied or elderly patient, fill in both. The emergency contact is who you phone; the next of kin is who can consent and be billed.
:::

## Identity documents and expiry

The document type recorded against a patient determines whether an expiry date is asked for.

When the document type is **Labour ID**, an **expiry date** field appears and should be filled in from the card itself.

### How an expired card is treated

| State | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| No expiry date recorded | The document is accepted, but nothing can be checked. |
| Expiry date in the future | Normal — no warning. |
| Expiry date passed | The card is flagged as expired and staff are warned. |

The check is applied both at registration and when the patient is next handled, so a card that lapses between visits is caught on the return visit rather than going unnoticed.

:::caution
An expired Labour ID usually affects the patient's entitlement to a scheme rate. Check the expiry before billing under a scheme, not after.
:::
